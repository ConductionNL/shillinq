# bookkeeping-ifrs-16-lease

## Purpose

Capture, classify, measure, and disclose lease contracts under IFRS 16 (and the converged Dutch/EU equivalent) so that shillinq becomes a credible bookkeeping engine for medium and large entities that have adopted IFRS — either voluntarily (NL BV with international parent) or because they are an EU-listed group, a CSRD-in-scope subsidiary that consolidates upward, or a regulated financial entity. Before IFRS 16 (effective 1 January 2019), most lease contracts disappeared off the balance sheet as "operating leases" — only the monthly lease expense hit P&L. IFRS 16 collapsed that distinction: virtually every lease longer than 12 months and worth more than ~EUR 5,000 must be capitalised, producing a Right-of-Use (RoU) asset and a corresponding Lease Liability at lease commencement. The result is a structurally larger balance sheet, front-loaded interest expense, straight-line depreciation, and a permanent disclosure obligation in the notes.

For a Dutch MKB+ entity, IFRS 16 typically affects: car leases (operational lease becomes capitalised), office and warehouse leases, copier/printer leases, ICT hardware leases, forklift and machinery leases, and increasingly cloud-infrastructure commitments where the customer controls a specific asset (dedicated server, dark fibre). The accounting is mechanically correct but operationally painful — the lessee has to track dozens or hundreds of contracts, each with its own incremental borrowing rate (IBR), term, payment schedule, indexation clauses, extension options, and modification events. A shillinq customer that today maintains lease registers in Excel will, the first time they need an audit, discover that the spreadsheet is wrong: the IBR is mis-applied, indexation has not been remeasured, an extension that became "reasonably certain" was not captured, and the disclosure table is incomplete.

This change introduces a first-class Lease register inside shillinq, an IFRS 16 classification engine, automatic generation of the RoU asset (linked to bookkeeping-fixed-assets-depreciation) and Lease Liability (in the general ledger), a reassessment workflow for remeasurement events, the mandatory IFRS 16 disclosure tables (maturity analysis, weighted-average IBR, expense breakdown), and full handling of modifications, partial terminations, abandonments, and the short-term / low-value exemptions. Transition support covers both the modified retrospective approach (most common, no comparative restatement) and the full retrospective approach (used by listed groups with peer-comparability needs).

The success criterion is: a shillinq tenant should be able to capitalise its full lease portfolio, generate audit-ready journal entries every period, produce the disclosure note without rework, and pass a Big-4 IFRS 16 audit walkthrough without the auditor reaching for Excel.

## Data Model

Five new schemas in the `bookkeeping` register, plus targeted extensions to existing schemas.

**`lease-contract`** is the master record. Attributes: lease number (sequential), counterparty (FK to organisations register, the lessor), description, asset class (vehicle / real-estate / IT-hardware / machinery / other), commencement date, end date, non-cancellable term in months, extension options (array of {months, exercise-likelihood, exercise-by-date}), termination options (array of {months-after-commencement, penalty-amount}), payment frequency (monthly / quarterly / annual / irregular), payment timing (in-advance / in-arrears), base payment amount, payment currency (ISO 4217), indexation clause (none / fixed-percent / CPI / sector-index), indexation reset frequency, residual-value guarantee, purchase option (price, exercise-likelihood), initial direct costs, lease incentives received, restoration obligation (estimated cost, discount rate), incremental borrowing rate at commencement (IBR%), IBR derivation method (group-policy / yield-curve / weighted-average / external-quote), IBR source document reference, classification (finance / operating-pre-IFRS16 / IFRS16-capitalised / short-term-exempt / low-value-exempt), classification rationale (free text + structured checklist), transition method (modified-retrospective / full-retrospective / N/A-post-transition), and a status enum (draft / active / modified / terminated / expired).

**`lease-payment-schedule`** is the per-period derivation, generated automatically from `lease-contract`. One row per payment period over the full reasonably-certain term. Attributes: lease (FK), period sequence, period start, period end, payment due date, contractual payment amount, payment currency, FX rate (if non-functional currency), opening lease liability, interest accrued (= opening liability × periodic IBR), payment applied (split into interest portion + principal portion), closing lease liability, opening RoU asset, depreciation charge, closing RoU asset, and a posted-to-GL flag with FK to the resulting journal entry batch.

**`lease-reassessment-event`** captures every modification or remeasurement during the lease life. Attributes: lease (FK), event date, event type (indexation-remeasurement / extension-option-reassessment / termination-option-reassessment / payment-modification / scope-modification / term-modification / IBR-reset / impairment / abandonment / partial-termination / full-termination), trigger description, old contract snapshot (JSON), new contract snapshot (JSON), remeasurement approach (catch-up-adjustment / prospective / separate-lease), revised IBR (if applicable, with rationale), pre-event lease liability, post-event lease liability, RoU asset adjustment, P&L impact (gain/loss on modification), supporting documents (files relation), approver (FK user), approval date, and posted-to-GL flag.

**`lease-disclosure-table`** is the period-end snapshot used to generate the IFRS 16 notes. One record per reporting period (typically annual). Attributes: period, total RoU asset by class (object: real-estate / vehicles / IT / other), total RoU additions in period, total RoU depreciation in period, total RoU disposals in period, closing RoU by class, total lease liability split current / non-current, maturity analysis (object with buckets: <1y, 1-2y, 2-3y, 3-4y, 4-5y, >5y), weighted-average IBR (by class), total interest expense, total short-term lease expense, total low-value lease expense, total variable lease expense, total sublease income, cash outflow for leases (operating + financing), and qualitative narrative fields.

**`lease-portfolio-exemption`** records the tenant's policy elections under IFRS 16.5 (short-term, ≤12 months) and IFRS 16.6 (low-value, asset value when new ≤ ~USD 5,000). Attributes: policy effective date, short-term-by-class election (object: applied yes/no per asset class), low-value-threshold (in functional currency), low-value-by-class election, policy approver (FK user), policy document (file), and superseded-by (self-FK for policy history).

**Extensions to existing schemas:** `fixed-asset` gains an `is-rou-asset` boolean and a `source-lease` FK so the depreciation engine treats RoU assets correctly and the asset register filters cleanly. `journal-entry-line` gains an optional `source-lease-event` FK so every IFRS 16 posting is traceable to a lease and an event. `general-ledger-account` gets a new flag `is-lease-account` and a sub-type enum (rou-real-estate / rou-vehicles / rou-it / rou-other / lease-liability-current / lease-liability-noncurrent / lease-interest-expense / lease-depreciation / short-term-lease-expense / low-value-lease-expense) so the disclosure-table generator can aggregate without relying on free-text account names.

## Requirements

**Lease detection and classification.** A wizard walks the user through the IFRS 16 decision tree on every new contract entered in the lease module — or, if the source is an existing contract uploaded to docudesk, an LLM extractor populates the wizard fields and the user only confirms. The wizard distinguishes IFRS 16 leases (any contract conveying the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period in exchange for consideration) from service contracts (no identified asset, or no control), then evaluates the short-term and low-value exemptions, then — for entities still on the old Dutch GAAP for some entities — classifies finance vs operating under the old rules. Every decision is recorded as a checklist in `classification-rationale` with a one-click "regenerate for changed circumstance" if the contract is later restructured.

**Right-of-use asset and lease liability at commencement.** When a contract reaches `active` status, the system computes the present value of the unavoidable payments over the reasonably-certain term using the per-contract IBR (or, if the rate implicit in the lease is readily determinable, that rate). The PV becomes the opening Lease Liability; the RoU asset opens at PV + prepayments + initial direct costs − lease incentives + estimated restoration obligation. Both postings flow into the GL via a single batched journal entry that also creates the corresponding `fixed-asset` record (so depreciation runs the normal monthly schedule with `straight-line` over the lease term, unless the contract grants ownership at end-of-term in which case the asset's useful life is used).

**Incremental borrowing rate.** The IBR is the single most material judgement in IFRS 16. The system supports four derivation methods. (1) Group-policy: tenant uploads a quarterly-refreshed IBR matrix (currency × tenor × asset-class) and the engine picks the cell. (2) Yield-curve: tenant provides risk-free curve points and a fixed credit spread; the system interpolates. (3) Weighted-average: the engine reuses the tenant's actual external debt cost weighted by maturity (pulled from the general ledger). (4) External-quote: a per-lease manual rate with a mandatory supporting-document attachment. Every IBR is timestamped, signed off by a named approver, and frozen against the lease — a later change is a reassessment event, not an edit.

**Periodic posting.** Every month-end (or at the tenant's chosen period), the engine generates one journal entry per lease covering: interest expense (Dr) / lease liability (Cr) for the interest portion; lease liability (Dr) / bank (Cr) for the cash payment; and depreciation (Dr) / accumulated-depreciation-RoU (Cr) for the RoU charge — the last of which is actually generated by the existing bookkeeping-fixed-assets-depreciation engine on its normal cycle, since the RoU asset is a `fixed-asset` row with `is-rou-asset=true`. Batched postings carry a `source-lease-event` reference and roll up into a single user-visible "IFRS 16 month-end" workflow step that must be approved before the period closes.

**Reassessment and remeasurement.** Indexation clauses are evaluated on every effective date (typically annually for CPI clauses); a non-zero delta triggers a `lease-reassessment-event` of type `indexation-remeasurement` that recomputes the schedule from the event date forward and adjusts the lease liability (Dr/Cr) against the RoU asset (Cr/Dr) with no immediate P&L impact. Extension-option reassessment is triggered manually (auditor-visible review every reporting period) or automatically when there is a "significant event or change in circumstance" the user flags (e.g., business decision to renew). Term modifications, scope modifications, and payment modifications follow the IFRS 16.44–46 decision tree: separate lease vs not-separate lease; if not separate, the discount rate is reset to the IBR at the modification date. Each path is implemented as a guided workflow, never as a free-text journal entry.

**Modifications, partial terminations, abandonments.** A partial termination (e.g., returning two floors of a five-floor lease) reduces both the liability and the RoU pro-rata, with the difference recognised in P&L as a gain or loss on modification. An abandonment (the asset is no longer used but the lease is not legally terminated) impairs the RoU asset down to recoverable value while leaving the liability intact and continuing the interest accrual. The reassessment workflow captures the supporting evidence (board decision, customer notification, sublease contract if any) and posts the multi-line journal entry atomically.

**Short-term and low-value exemptions.** Leases under 12 months at commencement, or covering assets with a new-asset value below the tenant's elected threshold (default USD 5,000 ≈ EUR 4,500), are posted as straight-line operating expense across the lease term. The exemption is contract-by-contract, but the policy election is portfolio-wide — the system enforces consistency by class.

**Disclosure table generation.** The `lease-disclosure-table` is generated on demand and on every period close. It produces: the IFRS 16.51 quantitative disclosures (depreciation by class, interest expense, short-term expense, low-value expense, variable expense, sublease income, total cash outflow, RoU additions, RoU carrying amount by class, lease liability maturity analysis at undiscounted-cash-flow basis) and the IFRS 16.59 qualitative narrative seeds (nature of the lessee's leasing activities, future cash outflows the lessee is exposed to but not yet reflected, restrictions or covenants imposed by leases, sale-and-leaseback transactions). The table is exportable to XBRL using the ESEF / EFRAG taxonomy via bookkeeping-sbr-xbrl-reporting.

**Transition.** A one-time transition wizard supports modified retrospective (the standard practical choice: existing operating leases are recognised at the date of initial application using the IBR at that date, with an optional practical expedient of measuring the RoU at the same amount as the liability less prepaid/accrued lease payments) and full retrospective (restating comparative periods as if IFRS 16 had always applied). The wizard records every practical expedient elected (IFRS 16.C3, C10) and generates the transition disclosure note. After transition, the wizard self-archives but remains read-only for audit purposes.

**Audit pack.** A one-click export produces: the complete lease register as PDF + CSV, all per-lease schedules, the IBR derivation evidence, every reassessment event with before/after snapshots, the disclosure table, the transition methodology, all supporting documents, and a sign-off page. Designed to be handed to a Big-4 senior associate.

## Standards & Sources

Primary standards: IFRS 16 *Leases* (effective 1 January 2019), IFRIC interpretations relevant to lease term and discount rate, IFRS 16 Illustrative Examples, EFRAG implementation guidance, and the IASB November 2023 amendments on sale-and-leaseback. EU adoption is via Commission Regulation (EU) 2017/1986. For Dutch entities not on IFRS, the equivalent national standard is RJ 292 (Richtlijnen voor de Jaarverslaggeving), which preserves the operating/finance distinction; shillinq supports both paths but defaults to IFRS 16 capitalisation for any entity that has elected IFRS-EU. For US-headquartered groups consolidating up, ASC 842 differences are documented but not automatically reconciled (Phase 2). Reference materials: KPMG *IFRS 16: Leases handbook*, PwC *In depth: A look at current financial reporting issues — IFRS 16*, Deloitte iGAAP *Leases* chapter, EY *International GAAP — Leases*, and the IFRS Foundation *Education Material on the Discount Rate*. For the IBR derivation methodology, the EFRAG discussion paper *Improving the Financial Reporting of Income Tax* and the Bank for International Settlements yield-curve datasets provide the empirical underpinning. Indexation handling follows the IFRS 16.27 / IFRIC May 2020 agenda decision on rent concessions. Disclosure layouts follow the ESEF mandatory iXBRL taxonomy and EFRAG's 2024 ESRS-cross-reference table where the leased-asset class overlaps with sustainability reporting (e.g., the carbon footprint of the leased vehicle fleet is sourced from this register for E1 reporting).

## Cross-app integration

**bookkeeping-general-ledger** is a hard dependency — every IFRS 16 transaction is a GL posting, and the new account sub-types must be seeded during register setup. **bookkeeping-fixed-assets-depreciation** is a hard dependency — RoU assets reuse the fixed-asset depreciation engine via the `is-rou-asset` flag, so the depreciation schedule, impairment workflow, and disposal workflow all work unchanged. **bookkeeping-sbr-xbrl-reporting** consumes the disclosure table to emit the ESEF iXBRL tags for KvK deposition. **bookkeeping-csrd-esrs** consumes the lease register for ESRS E1 (climate) Scope 1+2 calculations where the lessee is operationally responsible for the asset (company cars, leased buildings with direct energy bills). **docudesk** is the source of contract PDFs and is invoked via the LLM extractor to pre-populate the lease wizard. **openconnector** pushes IBR matrices in from the tenant's group treasury system or pulls central bank curves on a schedule. **organisations** holds the lessor counterparty records. **launchpad** surfaces a tenant-wide lease portfolio dashboard (total RoU, weighted IBR, upcoming reassessments, exemption-policy compliance). **opencatalogi** publishes the IFRS 16 capability under "regulatory reporting" with the relevant standard tags. **decidesk** routes every reassessment event over a configurable threshold (default EUR 100,000 RoU impact) through a board-decision workflow.

## Target users

The primary user is the **group financial controller** of a Dutch BV with EUR 20M–500M turnover that has either voluntarily adopted IFRS-EU (because a parent or PE owner requires it) or is in scope of CSRD and consolidates up into an IFRS group. Secondary users are the **lease administrator** (typically a senior bookkeeper who maintains the register, runs the wizard, and posts the month-end), the **treasurer** (owns the IBR policy and signs off the quarterly matrix), the **external auditor** (read-only access to the audit pack), and the **CFO / FD** (reviews the disclosure note and approves significant reassessment events). For the smaller end of the target market — companies that have just crossed the IFRS-EU threshold or have just been told by their new private-equity owner that next year's books will be IFRS — the value proposition is that shillinq replaces a EUR 30K/year external IFRS 16 tool (Visual Lease, LeaseAccelerator, Nakisa) bundled with the bookkeeping system the company already uses. For shillinq the strategic value is that IFRS 16 is the gateway feature that proves the platform can handle "real" financial reporting, opening the door to ESRS, SBR-XBRL, and ultimately full IFRS consolidation in later changes.
