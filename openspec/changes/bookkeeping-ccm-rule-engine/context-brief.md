# bookkeeping-ccm-rule-engine

## Purpose

Build a Continuous Controls Monitoring (CCM) rule engine that examines every journal entry, every master-data change, and every payment instruction at the point of posting (and again on a periodic sweep) against a configurable library of forensic and SOX-style controls — flagging, quarantining, or escalating anomalies in real time and producing a defensible findings dashboard with full audit-committee reporting. The objective is to replace the after-the-fact, sample-based, once-a-year work an external auditor or internal-audit function does today with a continuous, population-based, evidence-rich layer that runs every minute the books are open. For shillinq's enterprise target customer — a Dutch MKB+ at the upper end of the segment, often a foreign-owned subsidiary subject to parent-company SOX 404 controls, or a financial-services entity under DNB / AFM oversight, or simply a fast-growing company whose audit firm has flagged "tone-at-the-top" risk after a controls-weakness letter — CCM is the difference between a clean audit and a long, expensive findings list.

The motivation is concrete. Most fraud in MKB+ entities is committed by trusted insiders using the accounting system the company already trusts. The classic patterns are well known: a payment-clerk who creates a fictitious vendor master record and books invoices to it; a CFO who posts a manual journal entry on the last working day of the quarter to smooth revenue; a controller who approves their own expense reimbursement when the four-eyes rule is bypassable; a procurement manager who modifies a vendor's bank account immediately before a large payment; a sales operator who registers an unusually-large credit note minutes before month-end. Each pattern can be expressed as a rule. Each pattern, if you have a rule engine that fires on every journal entry as it is written, can be detected within milliseconds. None of these patterns are typically caught by today's MKB+ accounting systems, which are control-poor and rely on the external auditor's sample test once a year. CCM closes that gap.

This change introduces a Rule register inside shillinq containing a curated library of ~60 default controls grouped into eight families (segregation of duties, duplicate detection, anomalous-amount detection, timing anomalies, master-data changes, approval-bypass detection, manual-journal-entry forensics, value-chain integrity), a rule-evaluation pipeline that runs synchronously on every relevant event and asynchronously on a nightly sweep, a Findings register for triage, a four-state workflow (open / under-investigation / dismissed-with-rationale / confirmed-issue-escalated), an Audit Committee report generator, a trend-analysis layer, and a SOX-mode toggle that enables the additional documentation requirements (control owner, control frequency, control evidence, deficiency rating, remediation plan) that a SOX 404 attestation requires.

The success criterion is: a shillinq tenant adopting CCM should within the first month detect at least one anomaly worth investigating that would have otherwise passed undetected, and within the first year produce an audit committee that can sign off on internal controls over financial reporting with population-level evidence rather than sample-based testing.

## Data Model

Six new schemas in the `bookkeeping` register, plus extensions.

**`ccm-rule`** is the rule definition. Attributes: rule code (sequential within tenant, e.g., `CCM-001`), name, description (plain-language), control family (segregation-of-duties / duplicate-detection / anomalous-amount / timing / master-data / approval-bypass / manual-journal / value-chain / custom), control objective (free text, e.g., "prevent vendor master fraud"), control assertion (mapped to COSO assertions: existence / completeness / accuracy / valuation / rights-and-obligations / presentation-and-disclosure), severity (informational / low / medium / high / critical), evaluation mode (synchronous-block / synchronous-warn / asynchronous-detect), schedule (event-triggered / hourly / daily / weekly / monthly), trigger events (array: journal-entry-posted / vendor-created / vendor-modified / payment-instruction-created / approval-granted / period-end-approaching / etc.), rule logic (JSON expression in a constrained DSL — see Requirements), parameters (object — tenant-configurable thresholds), suppression rules (object — known exceptions that should not fire), enabled (boolean), effective-from, effective-to, rule owner (FK user, typically internal-audit or CFO), rule reviewer (FK user, typically external-auditor for SOX engagements), last-review-date, next-review-date, SOX-key-control (boolean), and version-history (self-FK chain).

**`ccm-finding`** is a single rule firing. Attributes: rule (FK), fire-timestamp, triggering event (typed reference — journal entry / payment / vendor change / etc.), severity (inherited from rule but overridable), title (auto-generated, e.g., "Manual JE EUR 487,000 posted by CFO on last day of Q4"), evidence (JSON snapshot of all relevant data at fire time: the journal entry lines, the user who posted, the related vendor master record, the approval chain, recent similar findings), suggested-investigation-steps (auto-generated from rule template), assignee (FK user, default = rule owner), status (open / under-investigation / awaiting-information / dismissed-false-positive / dismissed-acceptable-risk / confirmed-control-deficiency / confirmed-fraud-suspected), status-history (audit trail), investigation-notes (array of {timestamp, user, note}), resolution-rationale (mandatory free text on dismissal or confirmation), resolved-by (FK user), resolved-at (timestamp), escalated (boolean), escalated-to (FK user or role), escalated-at, linked-findings (array — for pattern detection across related fires), and external-reference (e.g., audit-firm finding number, whistleblower report).

**`ccm-segregation-matrix`** is the SoD reference data. Attributes: function code (e.g., `VENDOR-CREATE`, `INVOICE-POST`, `PAYMENT-RELEASE`, `BANK-RECONCILIATION`, `JOURNAL-MANUAL`, `MASTER-DATA-CHANGE`, `APPROVAL-EXPENSE`), conflicting functions (array of function codes), conflict severity (low / medium / high / critical), rationale, compensating-controls-allowed (array of control descriptions). One row per function code; the matrix is pre-loaded with the standard SAP / Oracle 50-function library and tenant-configurable. Effectively a graph of "no single user may hold both X and Y".

**`ccm-user-function-assignment`** is the materialised assignment of shillinq roles and granular permissions to the SoD function codes — computed nightly. Attributes: user (FK), function code, source (role / direct-grant / temporary-elevation), granted-at, granted-by, expires-at, conflict-with (array of {function, severity}). Used by the SoD rule and by the periodic SoD review report.

**`ccm-baseline`** stores the statistical baselines that anomalous-amount and Benford's-Law rules compare against. Attributes: baseline scope (vendor / GL-account / cost-centre / project / tenant-wide), baseline metric (mean / median / stddev / quartile / Benford-leading-digit-distribution / typical-day-of-week / typical-hour-of-day / typical-poster-set / etc.), period covered, sample size, computed value (typed), confidence interval, last-refresh-timestamp, refresh-cadence (daily / weekly / monthly), and stable (boolean — false if sample size or variance is too small to flag).

**`ccm-audit-committee-report`** is the period-end deliverable. Attributes: period, generated-at, generator (FK user), executive summary (auto-drafted, editable), rule-firings-by-family (object), rule-firings-by-severity (object), findings-by-status (object), unresolved-criticals (array of FK findings), top-N investigated findings (array), trend-analysis (year-over-year comparison), SoD violations summary, manual-JE summary, approver-bypass attempts, control deficiencies for SOX 404 (if SOX-mode-enabled), remediation status, recommendations, approver (FK user, typically audit-committee chair), approval date, and distribution log.

**Extensions:** `journal-entry` gains a `ccm-evaluation-status` enum (not-evaluated / evaluated-clean / evaluated-with-findings / evaluation-pending) and an `ccm-evaluation-timestamp`. `journal-entry-line` gains a `posting-channel` enum (system-generated / interface-import / manual-by-user / batch-correction / reversal) used heavily by manual-JE rules. `organisations` (used for vendor master) gains `master-data-change-log` (array of {timestamp, user, field, old-value, new-value}) and `bank-account-history` (array). `user` (Nextcloud user) gains a `risk-rating` (1-5) attribute derivable from role and finding history.

## Requirements

**Rule DSL.** Rules are expressed in a constrained JSON DSL — not arbitrary code — so that they are auditable, version-controllable, and safe to ship as seed data. Core operators: `event-matches` (matches trigger event), `field-equals`, `field-in-set`, `field-greater-than`, `field-between`, `field-matches-regex`, `time-is-within` (e.g., last day of period ±2), `time-is-weekend`, `time-is-outside-business-hours`, `user-has-function`, `user-also-has-function`, `user-is-in-role`, `value-deviates-from-baseline` (with z-score threshold), `value-violates-benford` (chi-square test), `count-of-similar-in-period-exceeds`, `duplicate-of-existing` (with fuzzy-match parameters), `master-data-changed-within` (lookback window), `approval-chain-bypassed`, `posted-while-period-closing`, `same-user-as` (back-reference). Compound rules combine with `all-of` / `any-of` / `none-of`. Parameters (thresholds, lookback windows, sample sizes) are tenant-configurable per rule instance — the rule library ships sensible defaults grounded in published forensic-accounting research.

**Synchronous evaluation.** When evaluation mode is synchronous-block or synchronous-warn, the rule fires during journal posting (or vendor save, or payment release). Block mode prevents the underlying transaction from completing and returns the finding to the user; warn mode allows it but flags it. Block-mode rules are used sparingly (typical examples: SoD violation on payment release, or a duplicate-invoice detection above a high-confidence threshold) and must have a documented override path with mandatory rationale captured into the finding.

**Asynchronous evaluation.** Asynchronous-detect rules run on a scheduled sweep (nightly is default) over the new transactions since the last sweep. Used for pattern-based rules that need a population to evaluate (Benford's Law, trend deviations, peer-group comparisons).

**Default rule library.** Sixty rules ship as seed data, grouped:

*Segregation of Duties.* SoD-01 same-user-created-and-paid-vendor; SoD-02 same-user-created-and-approved-invoice; SoD-03 approver-also-poster on a journal entry; SoD-04 reconciler-also-poster on bank reconciliation; SoD-05 master-data-change-by-AP-clerk-without-secondary-approval; SoD-06 dormant-account-reactivated-by-single-user; SoD-07 critical-role-held-by-single-user (no backup, business-continuity risk).

*Duplicate detection.* DUP-01 exact-duplicate-invoice (same vendor + amount + reference); DUP-02 fuzzy-duplicate-invoice (same vendor + amount within 1% + within 30 days); DUP-03 duplicate-payment (same vendor + bank + amount + within 7 days); DUP-04 duplicate-vendor (same bank account, different name / address / KvK); DUP-05 duplicate-employee (same bank account on two employee records).

*Anomalous amounts.* AMT-01 Benford's-Law violation on leading-digit distribution (chi-square > threshold over rolling 12-month window); AMT-02 round-number anomaly (suspicious frequency of "10,000.00" style amounts); AMT-03 just-under-threshold (e.g., invoice EUR 24,950 when approval threshold is EUR 25,000); AMT-04 amount-deviates-from-vendor-baseline (z-score > 3); AMT-05 amount-deviates-from-GL-account-baseline; AMT-06 vendor-spike (vendor total this month > 5x trailing-12-month average).

*Timing anomalies.* TIM-01 weekend-posting (manual JE posted Saturday or Sunday); TIM-02 outside-business-hours posting (manual JE between 20:00-06:00 local); TIM-03 last-day-of-period posting (manual JE on the last business day of the quarter, above threshold); TIM-04 backdated posting (entry date > 5 business days before posting date); TIM-05 forward-dated posting; TIM-06 period-end concentration (>30% of monthly manual JEs in the last 3 days).

*Master-data integrity.* MD-01 vendor bank account changed within 14 days of large payment; MD-02 vendor name change shortly after creation (potential layering); MD-03 vendor address matches employee address; MD-04 vendor phone matches employee phone; MD-05 vendor created and used same day (rush vendor); MD-06 dormant vendor reactivated for first payment in >12 months.

*Approval bypass.* AB-01 emergency-override invoked; AB-02 approval chain shorter than policy requires; AB-03 approver-on-leave but approved (timestamp during recorded absence); AB-04 self-approval (approver = requester); AB-05 split-PO (multiple POs to same vendor within 30 days that sum just above approval threshold).

*Manual JE forensics.* MJ-01 manual JE by C-suite without secondary approval; MJ-02 manual JE with one-sided narrative ("adjustment", "correction" with no detail); MJ-03 manual JE reversed within 5 days (potential covering); MJ-04 manual JE to revenue account; MJ-05 manual JE to reserves account; MJ-06 round-trip JE (Dr/Cr same account through suspense).

*Value-chain integrity.* VC-01 payment to high-risk country (per FATF / EU sanctions list); VC-02 payment to entity on EU sanctions list (auto-blocked); VC-03 vendor with PEP match (Politically Exposed Person); VC-04 customer with adverse-media match (continuous monitoring via openconnector to a screening provider); VC-05 invoice from new vendor without KvK / VAT validation.

**Finding workflow.** Every fire creates a finding; the assignee receives a notification; status starts at open; the assignee investigates and either dismisses (with rationale) or escalates. Critical-severity findings auto-escalate after 24h without response to a configurable escalation path (CFO, audit committee chair, etc.). Findings cluster — a rule that fires repeatedly on the same vendor or user is automatically grouped into an investigation thread.

**Audit committee reporting.** A configurable schedule (monthly / quarterly) auto-generates the Audit Committee report, which the CFO reviews and submits. The report includes the executive summary (LLM-drafted from the period's findings), trend tables, top findings, SoD compliance scorecard, manual-JE summary, deficiencies-and-remediation, and recommendations for new or modified rules. Distribution is tracked.

**Trend analysis.** Each rule's fire-rate over time, false-positive rate, dismissal rate, and average time-to-resolution are tracked. The trend layer detects rules that have drifted (suddenly firing 10x more) and flags them for re-tuning.

**SOX mode.** A tenant toggle activates the additional metadata SOX 404 requires: every rule becomes a documented control with owner, frequency, evidence, and last-test date; deficiencies are rated (control deficiency / significant deficiency / material weakness); remediation plans are tracked; the external auditor gets a scoped role with read access; a quarterly attestation workflow produces management's certification document.

**Performance.** Synchronous rules must add no more than 100ms latency to a journal posting at the 95th percentile; asynchronous sweeps must complete within the overnight window for a tenant with 1M+ journal lines per year. The DSL is compiled once into an evaluation plan and cached; baselines are materialised, not computed per evaluation.

## Standards & Sources

Primary frameworks: COSO *Internal Control — Integrated Framework* (2013) and COSO *Enterprise Risk Management — Integrating with Strategy and Performance* (2017) for the control taxonomy and the IRO linkage; PCAOB Auditing Standard 2201 *An Audit of Internal Control Over Financial Reporting* for SOX 404 evidence requirements; Sarbanes-Oxley Act §302 (management certification) and §404 (internal control attestation); for EU-listed equivalents, AFEP-MEDEF / Dutch Corporate Governance Code chapter on internal-risk-management-and-control. Standards on forensic detection: AICPA *Statement on Auditing Standards 99 / AU-C 240* on the consideration of fraud; Association of Certified Fraud Examiners *Fraud Examiners Manual* and the biennial *Report to the Nations*; ISACA *Auditing Continuous Controls Monitoring*; IIA *Practice Guide: Continuous Auditing*. Statistical techniques: Newcomb (1881) and Benford (1938) on leading-digit distributions, Nigrini (1996) on Benford's Law in accounting forensics, Durtschi-Hillison-Pacini (2004) on the application of Benford's Law to detecting fraud in accounting data. Segregation-of-duties libraries: SAP GRC standard ruleset (publicly documented function codes), Oracle Risk Management Cloud predefined access controls. Sanctions and screening: EU Consolidated Financial Sanctions List, OFAC SDN list, UN Consolidated Sanctions List (refreshed via openconnector), FATF high-risk and other monitored jurisdictions list, EU AML high-risk-third-countries list. Dutch supervisory expectations: DNB *Good Practice Document — Internal Audit Function*, AFM *Toezicht op governance*. Practitioner sources: Deloitte *Continuous controls monitoring — Going beyond a project mindset*, EY *Continuous Auditing & Continuous Monitoring*, KPMG *CCM: The new normal*, PwC *Risk and Compliance Continuous Auditing benchmark*. For payment-fraud patterns specifically: ACFE *Occupational Fraud and Abuse* and the work of ECB / NL Betalingsverkeer on APP fraud (Authorised Push Payment).

## Cross-app integration

**bookkeeping-journal-entries** is a hard dependency — every journal entry must trigger the synchronous rule evaluation. **bookkeeping-audit-trail** is a hard dependency — findings reference the audit trail's immutable event stream and CCM extends it with control-evaluation events. **bookkeeping-general-ledger** is used by GL-account-based rules and baseline computation. **bookkeeping-vendor-master** (within the wider bookkeeping suite) is the target of master-data-integrity rules. **bookkeeping-payments** (the payment-instruction pipeline) is where block-mode payment-release rules intercept. **bookkeeping-csrd-esrs** uses CCM to enforce its sustainability-data control objectives (e.g., "no ESRS data point approved without evidence"). **bookkeeping-ifrs-16-lease** uses CCM to enforce that reassessment events above a materiality threshold pass through board review. **openconnector** is the channel for sanctions-list, PEP-list, and adverse-media screening; for emission-factor and other external baselines. **organisations** holds the vendor and customer master that master-data rules guard. **docudesk** stores evidence attached to findings (invoices, contracts, screening reports). **decidesk** routes escalated findings into board governance workflows. **launchpad** publishes the CCM dashboard (findings by severity, SoD compliance scorecard, top firing rules, trend chart). **opencatalogi** publishes the CCM capability with COSO and SOX tags. **larpingapp** (Conduction's training platform) hosts the control-awareness training tied to CCM rule families. **n8n** can be wired to push critical findings to Slack / Teams / email beyond shillinq's in-app notifications.

## Target users

The primary user is the **internal audit lead** or **head of internal control** in a larger MKB+ entity (typically EUR 50M+ turnover, often a foreign subsidiary) — a role that is increasingly mandatory under DNB rules for financial-services entities and under the Dutch Corporate Governance Code for listed entities. In smaller tenants the role is held part-time by the **CFO** or by the **financial controller**. Secondary users are the **CFO** (reviews and signs the audit committee report), the **audit committee chair** (consumes the report and engages on critical findings), the **external auditor** (granted scoped read access for SOX or ISA 315 / ISA 330 testing), the **board** (for material weaknesses requiring disclosure), and individual **process owners** (AP manager, AR manager, treasurer) whose teams are the subject of many findings. Suppliers and customers do not interact with CCM directly but appear in findings related to value-chain integrity. For shillinq the strategic value is twofold: CCM is a hard differentiator at the upper end of the segment, where competitors (Exact Globe+, Twinfield, AFAS Profit) have no equivalent and the alternative is a separate EUR 50–150K/year tool (ACL/Galvanize, CaseWare, MindBridge); and CCM is the natural foundation for the next layer — AI-driven anomaly detection — which can ride on top of the same event stream once the rule-engine foundation is in place.
